---
title: The river on foot
date: 2026-08-19
place: The Danube Bend, Hungary
tags: [walking, rivers]
summary: Two days along the river where it turns south, with a castle, a ferry and far too many apricots.
---

The river is wide and brown and in no hurry, and at the bend it turns
south between wooded hills as if it had only just made up its mind. A path
runs along the bank, sometimes on a dyke, sometimes through orchards,
sometimes on the edge of a road where you walk in single file and wave at
lorries.

On the first day I climbed to a ruined castle above the bend. From the top
the river looks like a letter someone started to write and then crossed out.
There were swallows in the empty windows and a man selling cold water from a
cool box who had, he said, climbed up every day that summer.

Every village on the way had an apricot stall. I bought a bag in the first
one out of politeness and then could not stop. By the evening I had eaten so
many that I could not face the fish soup the guest house was famous for, and
the owner was offended until I explained.

On the second day I crossed by a little car ferry that runs whenever there is
somebody on the jetty, walked the other bank back upstream, and caught a
train home from a station that had a station cat and nothing else.
